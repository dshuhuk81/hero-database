Refinement Effect

Every refine is a weighing, not a dice roll.

Your artifact's twelve possible stats each sit on a scale at weight 1000. Materials add and subtract weight. The game then hands out your Awakened Effects in proportion. Understand the scale and the whole system stops being random.

The mechanism

One formula runs the entire system

There is no hidden pity, no streak protection, no per-material RNG. There is a weight per stat, and a division.

chance of a stat = its weight × open slots ÷ total weight
“Open slots” is your Awakened Tier minus the effects you've locked. A Tier 4 artifact with nothing locked has 4 open slots, so all twelve chances add up to 400 % — you are drawing four stats, not one.

Two rules bend that formula at the extremes, and between them they explain every strange number you have seen in the game:

The 1 % floor

Any stat that falls below 1 % is removed from the pool entirely. Its weight leaves the total, so everything else gets more likely. This is how a single strong material can force a guaranteed result — not by being big, but by making its rivals irrelevant.

The 100 % pin

Any stat that reaches 100 % is locked in, removed from the pool, and the open-slot count drops by one. The formula then runs again for the stats that are left. Pinning is sequential, which is why the last slot behaves so differently from the first.

Rule one

Your artifact's slot decides which stats exist

Not the Tier. Not whether it is Hefty, Nimble or Arcane. The slot, and only the slot. An artifact equipped in Slot 2 has no CRIT Rate in its pool — no amount of refining will ever produce it. Tier only decides how many of the twelve you get to hold.

Rule two

Two kinds of material, wildly different power

Spare artifacts nudge the scale. Refine stones move it. Knowing the ratio saves you from feeding your inventory into a rounding error.

Spare artifacts as fodder

12 profiles · quality only scales them

Every artifact pushes two stats up hard, two up gently, and two down hard — determined purely by its type and slot. There are only twelve distinct profiles in the whole game. At quality 13 that is +200 / +100 / −200, with −40 to everything else.

Icons are the quality‑13 art. Lower qualities are the same object in a plainer frame, with proportionally smaller weights.

Below quality 12, don't bother. A quality‑8 artifact moves a stat by roughly one percentage point. Twenty quality‑13 artifacts raise your expected hits on a four-stat goal from 1.33 to about 2.07 — real, but nowhere near a guarantee.
CRIT Rate and P‑DMG fight each other. An Arcane artifact from Slot 1 is the only +200 source for CRIT Rate and it is −200 on P‑DMG. The +100 alternative, Hefty artifact from Slot 1, is −200 on CRIT DMG. No fodder raises CRIT Rate for free.
Rule three

The last open slot costs more than the first three combined

This is the single most expensive thing most players don't know. With several slots open, a stat needs only a modest weight to pin. With one slot open, the pin formula becomes impossible — the only remaining route is to push all eleven rivals under the 1 % floor, which needs a weight of 89 001.

The playbook

How to actually build the artifact you want

Two routes, because nobody has confirmed a source for refine stones. Start with the one you can run today.

Route A — spare artifacts only

works today · no refine stones needed

  1. Take the artifact to Tier 4 before you refine anything

    This is free value and the biggest single lever on the page. Four open slots put every target at 33.3 % before you add one material — at Tier 1 it is 8.3 %. Awakening Stones come from idle drops; craft upward 3-to-1 if you are short of a tier.

  2. Feed only quality 12–13 fodder, and only families that agree with each other

    For a Slot 1 physical DPS, use a Nimble artifact from Slot 1: +200 to ATK % and +200 to P‑DMG at once, with both of its penalties landing on stats you weren't chasing. Ten of them puts ATK % and P‑DMG at 84.5 % each with four slots open.

  3. Reject rolls you don't like

    Refining Stones drop from idle rewards from campaign stage 3001, so retrying is cheap. You lose the loaded fodder, not your progress.

  4. Lock last, not first

    Every Fate lock costs you an open slot and makes what remains far harder. Lock only when you have stopped refining that artifact.

  5. Only now spend Hammers on levels

    Refining re-rolls each effect's enhancement level, drawn from 1 up to its current value. Every level bought before the stats are final is thrown away.

Honest expectation: around two of four target stats. Twenty quality‑13 artifacts move the expected hit count from 1.33 to 2.07. Fodder is a nudge, and the nudge has a ceiling.

Route B — if refine stones ever become available

worked with T3 · substitute your tier in the simulator

  1. Tier 4 first, same as above

    Pinning one stat needs 1 T3 stone at four open slots, and 23 at one open slot.

  2. Refine with stones on three of your four targets

    Two T3 stones per target — six total — pins all three at 100 %. The fourth effect is a free random draw from the nine left over.

  3. Fate-lock those three

    Three of item 1035, refunded when you unlock. Effectively free; you just need three at once.

  4. Refine again for the fourth

    One slot open, so this is the expensive one — 23 T3 stones guaranteed, or fewer and retry.

Pitfalls

Four ways to waste a lot of material

  • Don't lock early. Every Fate lock removes one open slot, and fewer open slots make everything that remains dramatically more expensive. Lock only between the two staged refines.
  • Don't refine an artifact you've already enhanced. Refining re-rolls each effect's level, drawn from 1 up to its current level — so it halves your invested Hammers on average and can never raise them.
  • Don't trust the Quick Apply button. It optimises only the stat you have filtered and happily loads fodder that pushes your three other targets down.
  • Don't read “89 % each” as “89 % for the set”. Those are per-stat chances. Eight T4 stones spread across four targets shows 89 % per stat but lands all four together only about 43 % of the time.
Provenance

Where these numbers come from, and what is still uncertain

Sources
Global client com.goatgames.mot.gb.gp, config snapshot 2026‑08‑07, client logic from the 2026‑07‑31 build. The probability model is a direct port of EquipAwakeAttrProbCalculator.calcInner. Stat pools, material weights and stone values come from equip_awake.json, equip_detail.json and item.json. These files are byte-identical in every Global build since June 2026 and in the CN client, so everything here applies to both regions.
A bug in the game: refine stones don't suppress rivals
Each stone's data carries a second value — a penalty it should apply to the other eleven stats. The client builds that penalty map keyed by list position instead of stat id, so the calculator discards it. In practice a stone only ever adds to its own stat. This is present in every Global and CN build inspected. Spare artifacts are unaffected; their penalties do land, which is why fodder can push a stat down to 1.8 % but stones never can. All numbers on this page use the behaviour the game actually ships.
What isn't settled
Server behaviour on that bug. The client provably drops the penalty; whether the server applies it is untestable from the files. One experiment settles it — load exactly one T1 stone on a Tier‑1 artifact: 15.4 % means the shipped model, 16.1 % means otherwise.

Refine stone sources. None. All 13 config snapshots were scanned for reward-shaped references and then, separately, for the bare item ids in every field of every file — the only hits are collisions with stage ids, shop row ids and level numbers. Odyssey was checked specifically after a player report; its rewards are four unrelated items plus gems. That report turned out to be about the Awakening Stones, which are a different family entirely and are fully sourced. So either refine stones are granted by something the client never sees, or they are not released.

How the draw is realised. The calculator produces per-stat chances; how the server turns four chances into four distinct stats isn't in the client. Anything pinned at 100 % is certain under any scheme, so the planning numbers hold — the sub‑100 % figures are the client's own display values.
