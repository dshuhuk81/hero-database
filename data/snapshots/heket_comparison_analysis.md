# Heket combat comparison — conventional versus summon formations

## Executive verdict

Heket is **summon-specialized, not summon-dependent**. She can operate in a formation with no other summoner because she creates her own frogs; those frogs activate her ATK/healing aura, receive her Ultimate shield, croak for damage and Hit reduction, and can be sacrificed to prevent her death. That makes her usable outside a summon composition.

She is not, however, a broadly versatile support for conventional heroes. In the no-summoner recording, every point of Heket-sourced effective healing went to Heket or her frogs, and her recorded ATK-aura/shield beneficiaries were likewise Heket and frogs. Her Lua selects friendly summons, not ordinary allied heroes. Thus, in a conventional team she occupies a support slot while mostly supporting her own subsystem.

The two summon recordings show the intended payoff. Heket healed 4.507–5.894 billion in total, with **3.680–4.849 billion (about 81.7–82.3%) going to allied summons outside Heket/frogs**. Her first Ultimate shield covered fourteen summon-class recipients in the Eris–Nuwa–Serket–Zeus run, versus four recipients in the no-summoner run. The longer Eris–Nuwa–Serket–Nüba run produced twenty-five shield recipient-applications over two Ultimates. This is genuine team support rather than merely extra personal damage.

The correct roster conclusion is therefore:

| Formation | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No summoner allies | **Usable niche, usually replaceable** | self-contained frogs, sustain, Hit pressure and lethal insurance; almost no direct support to ordinary allied heroes |
| One reliable summon engine | **Good conditional support** | external bodies become real ATK, healing and shield beneficiaries |
| Several durable/persistent summon engines | **Core specialist** | most of her kit scales across many recipients and her support footprint becomes team-wide |
| Enemies that erase or profit from summons | **Risky** | frog loss simultaneously removes damage, healing, debuff pressure and death insurance |

## Runtime implementation and captured parameters

### Symbiotic summon aura — `H_HaiKuiTe_skill0_Model`

On any friendly summon creation, Heket marks her summon count dirty. On update she recounts live entries in her summon manager. If the count is non-zero, she applies an ATK buff to herself and every friendly summon found by `findPlayerByFriendSummon`; every configured interval she heals the same population.

The captures expose:

- `atkBuffId = 30124103`
- `atkRate = 409` fixed point ≈ **39.94% ATK per live summon**
- `cureRate = 307` fixed point ≈ **29.98% of Heket's ATK per live summon**
- `cureTimeLength = 2048` = **2 seconds**

At the three-summon state observed here, the nominal input is about +119.8% ATK and a healing coefficient near 89.9% of Heket ATK per pulse. These are captured CN runtime values; they differ from the external database prose. Runtime behavior takes precedence for these fights.

The aura is the main answer to the versatility question: it does not select ordinary heroes. Without allied summons, Heket buffs/heals herself and frogs. With allied summons, it becomes a formation-scale engine.

### Summon active and classification — `H_HaiKuiTe_skill3_Model`

Heket is marked `MarkAsSummoner`, allowing summon-targeted effects to include her without turning her into a disposable minion for victory/revival rules. Her active creates frogs and applies buff `30121103`, whose captured exposed value was:

- **1,508,669,780 per recipient** in A and B1;
- **1,538,699,252 per recipient** in B2.

This value is the created shield buff's exposed work value, not verified absorbed damage. Deduplicated shield coverage was:

| Run/cast | Recipients | Nominal created shield across recipients |
|---|---:|---:|
| A, 17.956 s | Heket + 3 frogs = 4 | 6,034,679,120 |
| B1, 17.956 s | Heket + 13 friendly summons = 14 | 21,121,376,920 |
| B2, 17.956 and 35.376 s combined | 25 recipient-applications | 38,467,481,300 |

These are gross application values. Refresh/overlap and remaining shields are not settlement-equivalent absorption. Nevertheless, the recipient expansion is direct proof of Heket's support scaling: the same cast distributes over three times as many shield packages in B1 as in A.

The runtime cap replacement rule applies only to Heket-owned frogs. If a newly created frog puts her over `maxCount`, the lowest-current-HP older frog is force-killed. Allied summons increase aura beneficiaries but do not count against Heket's frog cap.

### Frog croak — `H_HaiKuiTe_Frog_skill1_Model`

When one frog begins its croak, it forces every other live frog to use the synchronized croak skill. Synchronized croaks do not recursively chain. Database behavior adds a local 3 m AoE and stacking Hit reduction.

Observed owned-frog damage:

- A: 22.246 million, 5.35% of Heket-system damage;
- B1: 28.044 million, 6.89%;
- B2: **273.064 million, 31.29%**.

B2 recorded twelve frog skill starts versus two in A and B1. That is the clearest offensive ceiling: durable frog uptime changes Heket from mostly basic attacks into a meaningful synchronized AoE/debuff source. Because B1 did not reproduce it, this ceiling is encounter-state dependent rather than guaranteed merely by selecting summoner allies.

Captured debuff `301212101` was applied to enemies during frog damage. This is consistent with the croak's Hit-reduction payload. A applied it to Prometheus twice; B1 reached Prometheus and Taurus; B2 produced repeated applications into Prometheus. Exact stack uptime cannot be reconstructed from the duplicated add/remove hook without buff-instance lifecycle IDs.

### Lethal protection — `H_HaiKuiTe_skill2_Model`

On lethal damage, if cooldown is ready and a Heket-owned frog is alive, Heket adds shield `30123104`, force-kills the lowest-HP frog, and returns false from `canDead`. Captured exposed shield value in A/B1 was **2,534,895,512**. The runtime cooldown is 30 seconds.

This is self-protection only. It does not save another hero. It is also coupled to the same frogs powering Heket's aura and croaks: a save preserves Heket but weakens the rest of her engine. A and B1 ultimately record Heket's death at 29.279 and 28.743 s. B2 records no Heket death, but no relic-shield application either; its lower incoming pressure avoided the lethal check.

### Exclusive equipment — `H_HaiKuiTe_skill4_Model`

The static Lua can add opening frogs, increase frog cap and reduce the relevant cooldown. `skill4` was listed as static evidence but did not instantiate in any of these three captures. The observed feature set was `skill0`, `skill2`, `skill3` plus frog `skill1`. Do not claim an exclusive-equipment proc or upgraded cap from these fights.

## Timeline and summon-count behavior

### A — conventional formation

- 0–6.097 s: zero live summons; first aura tier appears at 6.097 s.
- 6.097–16.750 s: one live summon; Heket's calculator/basic pressure rises with the counter.
- 16.750 s: two live summons.
- 17.487–17.956 s: first Ultimate sequence; counter reaches three and shield packages are applied.
- 22.244–23.316 s: frogs die and count falls from three to one.
- 27.939 s: Heket has rebuilt to two.
- 29.279 s: Heket dies with two summons recorded in the preceding state.

Heket successfully self-started, but the system remained a closed loop: all 1.812 billion healing went to Heket/frogs, and ordinary teammates received none of her aura.

### B1 — summon/Zeus formation

- Counter progression initially matches A: one at 6.097 s, two at 16.750 s, three at 17.956 s.
- The critical difference is beneficiary breadth, not earlier cap timing. At the Ultimate, fourteen summon-class units—including Heket, frogs, Nuwa figurines, Eris puppets, Serket scorpions and Zeus's clone—receive the shield.
- External summons receive 4.849 billion effective healing.
- Heket's personal damage remains approximately flat versus A.
- Heket dies at 28.743 s after multiple frog losses.

This run proves that even when personal DPS does not improve, Heket can still deliver much greater formation value through summon sustain and shield distribution.

### B2 — summon/Nüba formation

- Surprisingly, the counter remains zero through 12.931 s, reaches one at 17.219 s, then three at 17.956 s. “Summoner lineup” does not guarantee an immediate aura start; summon cast timing matters.
- Once established, three summons persist through 35.376 s.
- Heket casts a second Ultimate at 34.907–35.376 s.
- Twelve frog skill starts and 273.064 million frog damage are recorded.
- Heket receives only 1.527 billion incoming calculator damage and does not die during the capture.

This is the high-upside case: stable bodies extend Heket's life, allow a second Ultimate, multiply croaks, and produce 872.576 million total system damage. It also shows why duration and survival must be separated from raw totals.

## 1. Who enabled Heket most?

### Fight-verified

1. **Nuwa, Eris and Serket collectively — observed class identities plus event attribution.** Their figurines, puppets and scorpions were direct recipients of Heket's ATK buff, healing and shield. The capture cannot isolate which master alone contributed the most aura uptime because summon ownership was missing during early `PLAYER_LINEUP` initialization.
2. **Zeus in B1 — observed.** His clone received Heket healing/buffs/shield and broadened the summon population, but B1 did not improve Heket's personal DPS or survival over A.
3. **Nüba composition slot in B2 — observed composition difference, causal attribution uncertain.** B2 is the only run where Heket survives, casts twice and achieves high frog damage. Because this is a one-run composition change plus combat variance, do not claim Nüba alone caused the improvement.
4. **Heket's own frogs — observed and Lua-confirmed self-enablement.** They activate her aura in A, generate croaks, accept shields/heals and fuel lethal protection.

No ordinary ally is recorded directly healing, shielding or energizing Heket in a way attributable to a specific source in this comparison table. The question tested Heket's contribution to formations, not a controlled one-partner replacement, so partner rankings remain provisional.

### Mechanically recommended candidates

- **Nuwa — database-derived and fight-verified as a beneficiary.** Three differentiated figurines provide durable bodies, frontline relief, CC and healing; all can consume Heket's summon-specific support.
- **Eris — database-derived and fight-verified as a beneficiary.** Multiple puppets turn Heket's per-summon ATK/healing/shield into repeated value. Puppet churn can feed enemy death mechanics.
- **Serket — fight-verified.** Multiple scorpions dramatically increase recipients, though disposable bodies can die before converting all shield/heal value.
- **Zeus — fight-verified but not proven superior.** His clone qualifies, yet the B1 outcome shows one more summon source does not automatically solve Heket survival or DPS.
- **Any durable early summon master — Lua-confirmed category recommendation.** Early, persistent bodies matter more than the label “summoner”; B2 did not begin counting until late despite several summon-capable heroes.

## 2. What Heket gave teammates

### Ordinary heroes

Direct measured contribution is minimal. Her aura finder selects friendly summons. The masters—Nuwa, Eris, Serket, Zeus and the conventional heroes in A—do not appear as Heket-heal beneficiaries. Heket does provide indirect targeting relief through extra bodies and enemy Hit reduction, but the capture cannot convert those into exact prevented-damage totals.

### Allied summons

This is her true support audience:

- approximately 40% ATK per live summon tier via buff `30124103`;
- periodic healing every 2 s, scaling with live summon count;
- large shield packages on her Ultimate;
- local enemy Hit reduction from frog croaks;
- more bodies and targeting dilution from her own frogs.

Measured external-summon beneficiaries include Nuwa's tank/warrior/support figurines, Eris puppets, Serket scorpions and Zeus's clone. B1's largest effective-healing beneficiaries were Eris puppets (1.135b combined), Serket scorpions (1.083b), Nuwa's tank (1.031b), Zeus's clone (805.2m), Nuwa's warrior (710.0m), and Nuwa's support figurine (84.6m). B2 delivered 1.682b to Eris puppets, 1.265b to Nuwa's tank and 733.2m to Serket scorpions; recorded heals to two Nuwa figurines were fully overheal/zero effective.

This distinction matters: gross casts can be valuable even when effective healing is zero, because full-health summons still receive ATK buffs and shields. Effective healing measures recovered HP, not total support eligibility.

## 3. Damage specialization

Heket's own basic attack (`301251`) remains the primary path in A and B1: 94.65% and 93.11% of Heket-system damage. B2 is qualitatively different: basics fall to 68.71% while frog skills supply 31.29%.

Her damage specialization is sustained summon-count-scaled pressure, not immediate burst. Amplification priorities are:

1. establish summons before the early basic-attack sequence;
2. keep frogs alive long enough to synchronize croaks;
3. keep enemy clusters within frog 3 m range;
4. protect Heket through the 30 s relic cooldown;
5. only then add generic damage amplification or resistance shred.

The same enemies do not guarantee the same incoming targeting or summon survival. B1 versus B2 demonstrates that raw personal damage is highly sensitive to battlefield state. Do not sell Heket as a direct DPS upgrade solely because a lineup contains summoners.

## 4. Support specialization

Heket converts summon quantity into summon quality. Her unique specialty is simultaneously scaling ATK, healing and shielding across a population that most ordinary supports treat as secondary or disposable. She also converts frogs into one lethal-damage cancellation and synchronized accuracy pressure.

Ideal encounters are long enough to establish summons, allow bodies to remain grouped near enemies, and do not repeatedly erase all summons with unavoidable AoE. Her worst environments are short burst fights, summon-cleave, heal block, banish, enemies insensitive to Hit reduction, or mechanics that profit from unit deaths.

What she does **not** provide in the reviewed runtime:

- general healing to ordinary allied heroes;
- cleanse;
- universal damage reduction;
- Energy generation for teammates;
- CC immunity;
- resurrection;
- reliable hard crowd control.

That missing conventional-support package explains why she is usually replaceable outside summon teams even though her personal frog system works.

## Positioning, resources and survivability

Heket should be protected in the mid/back line while frogs and allied summons occupy forward space. Her basic attack repeatedly targets the current frontline enemy; frogs approach nearest enemies and require proximity for croaks. Wide enemy spacing reduces synchronized AoE value.

Her real resource is not Energy alone; it is the live summon population. Energy spending was identical through the shared window (1,024,000), leading to one Ultimate in all three. The differentiator was what that Ultimate could affect and whether the summon network survived afterward.

Survivability is coupled to frog availability. A and B1 take 6.843b and 11.259b incoming damage by the shared cutoff and die around 29 s. B2 takes only 1.527b, retains three summons, survives, and reaches a second Ultimate. Extra allied bodies may redirect targeting, but because B1 still dies, “more summons = Heket always survives” is not supported.

## Investment breakpoints

- **Aura ATK level:** the central summon-team breakpoint because every qualifying body receives the per-count scaling.
- **Periodic-heal level:** high value in long summon attrition; low value when summons are one-shot or remain at full HP.
- **Ultimate summon shield:** directly scales with recipient count, making it disproportionately valuable in dense summon formations.
- **Exclusive starting frogs/cap:** theoretically fixes the zero-count opening and improves self-sufficiency, but was not instantiated here.
- **Relic shield/cooldown:** useful only if at least one owned frog is alive at lethal damage; allied summons cannot be sacrificed by this code.

## Counters, anti-synergies and failure modes

1. Delete Heket's owned frogs before lethal pressure; allied summons do not substitute for the relic sacrifice.
2. Burst Heket twice inside the 30 s protection cooldown.
3. Use unavoidable AoE or summon-specific damage to collapse all aura beneficiaries together.
4. Spread enemies so frog croaks hit one target or spend time walking.
5. Apply heal block; much of her summon-team value is periodic recovery.
6. Exploit forced frog replacement/death with soul, on-death or kill-trigger mechanics.
7. End the fight before her first Ultimate around 17.5 s; without opening-frog investment, her best shield distribution never occurs.

## Final answer: versatility versus summon dependence

Heket is mechanically functional without summon allies, and A proves more than token activity: 415.8m Heket-system damage, 1.812b effective self/frog healing, four large Ultimate shield packages, frog Hit debuffs, and a 2.535b relic shield were all produced from her own kit. She is therefore not dead weight in a conventional formation.

But those numbers mostly recycle value inside Heket and her frogs. Her ordinary teammates received no measured direct healing or summon-aura buff. A conventional support slot normally needs to keep or amplify the actual heroes. Unless extra bodies, Hit reduction or her personal survivability specifically counter the encounter, Heket is usually not the efficient general-purpose choice.

In summon formations her role changes fundamentally. She distributed billions of healing to allied summons and multiplied the number of shield/ATK-buff beneficiaries. That support expansion appeared in both summon variants even when personal damage did not improve. Therefore her dependable selling point is **formation-wide summon support**, while the high personal damage seen in B2 is an upside case dependent on summon timing, survival and battlefield pressure.

**Recommendation:** treat Heket as a niche self-contained unit in non-summon teams, a strong conditional support with one durable summon engine, and a core specialist in persistent multi-summon teams. Do not describe her as universally versatile, and do not describe her as unusable without summoners; the evidence supports the narrower middle position.

## Coverage limitations

- Calculator events are not settlement totals; no settlement-screen reconciliation is present.
- Exposed shield values measure creation/application, not verified absorbed damage.
- Nested buff hooks duplicate raw entries; this report deduplicates known applications.
- Summon creation callbacks can be observed by multiple feature listeners; raw callback count is not physical summon count.
- B1 and B2 differ by one hero and are not repeated trials of an identical composition.
- Opponent metadata is a placeholder even though stage and enemy runtime evidence are consistent.
- No `skill4` feature instantiated, so exclusive-equipment behavior remains static-Lua evidence only.
- Hit-debuff stack uptime, avoided attacks, targeting relief and settlement-level team DPS are not exactly measurable from these logs.
